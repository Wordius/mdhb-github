An occasional post[^1] from [*The Markdown Handbook*](https://md-handbook.com/).

Among today’s links[^2] are markdown editors[^3] Editorial and SiYuan, Eziwiki, a lightweight wiki generator, KovaMD, a presentation app, and Agenda, a note-taking app tied to your calendar.

- [Editorial](https://www.omz-software.com/editorial/) is a plain text editor for iOS with markdown support and a number of powerful automation tools. It was, and still is, the best markdown editor I have ever come across on iOS, especially on the iPad. But there is one major issue: its continuing reliance on Dropbox, especially after what was dubbed the [Condoleezza effect](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/technology-26987980); there were also Dropbox’s various security issues to contend with. For these reasons, plus the fact that its developer failed to adopt iCloud, I stopped using Editorial some time ago. As to the app itself, Editorial offers folding sections, snippets, templates, an in-app browser and beautiful typography; I could go on. Available for iOS, priced at £4.99.
- [SiYuan](https://github.com/siyuan-note/siyuan), a personal knowledge management system, supports fine-grained block-level reference and markdown WYSIWYG. It is similar to Logseq, another app that uses a database. It also brings to mind one person who had just [lost six years of work](https://www.reddit.com/r/logseq/comments/1u4vw16/forced_to_leave_logseq_after_6_years_corrupt_db/) after his Logseq database corrupted. His big plan: switch to SiYuan. He may well lose another bunch of notes a few years down the line. Don’t rely on databases, not without a good backup plan in place. Hell, don’t rely on file-based storage without having a good backup plan in place. There are lifetime subscriptions starting at free, while $64 gets you backup with S3 support and $148 gets the official data sync/backup. Available for macOS, Linux and Windows. There are also apps for Android and iOS, the latter available under TestFlight. 
- [Eziwiki](https://github.com/i3months/eziwiki) is a modern lightweight wiki and documentation generator that is local and browser-based. It allows you to write content in markdown, configure navigation with TypeScript and deploy anywhere as static files. Node.js, 18.0 or higher, is required to install.
- [KovaMD](https://kova.md/) is free and open source desktop presentation app with no account required. A Kova presentation comprises a single markdown file, with slides separated by `---` on its own line. There are 11 built-in themes, syntax highlighting, presenter notes (notes added to slides but hidden from the viewers), PowerPoint and PDF exports, Mermaid diagrams and math, so it sounds like a useful app to have to in your toolbox. Available for macOS, Linux and Windows.
- [Agenda](https://www.agenda.com/) is a date-focused note-taking app. You don’t get to choose the font unless you have paid for the app, so I’m afraid free users are stuck with Avenir. It allows you to copy notes as markdown, rtf, html and plain text, and export in a handful of other options, including PDF. One thing you will notice in the menus is that many of the features are tied into Agenda’s premium offering so there’s no way to access them without paying, which is standard fare for a paid app. But it costs £39.99 per year; yes, you read that right, *per year*. Available for macOS.
